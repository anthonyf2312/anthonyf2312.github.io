What a unit is

A unit is a card you put in your five-card deck. During a match you cannot summon it on demand — you pay mana and one of your five cards appears at random. So a unit is less "a tower you place" and more "one of five things that might show up".

Each unit has a rank on the board, raised by merging and reset when the match ends, and a level on the card itself, raised permanently with gold outside matches. Two different numbers, easy to confuse — and some units interact with rank directly, so the distinction matters more than it first appears.

What rarity actually means

There are four tiers: Common, Rare, Epic and Legendary. Rarity is mainly a statement about supply and cost — it controls three things:

Rarity controlsWhat that means in practice
Which chests it appears inRarer cards show up less often, so duplicates are slower to collect
How often the shop offers itYou see rare cards for sale less
What upgrades costRarer cards cost more gold per level — and legendaries additionally need magic orbs

Legendaries are worth investing in. On top of what they do on the board, upgrading legendary units is one of the two things that raises your legendarity — the stat that unlocks spells, artifacts and profile components. No amount of levelling a Common does that.

What rarity really costs you is time: duplicates arrive slowly and legendaries additionally need magic orbs at every level, so a legendary climbs slower than a common even when you have the gold for it.

Factions

Every unit belongs to a faction. Five are documented here so far:

  • Dark Domain
  • Forest Alliance
  • Kingdom of Light
  • Magic Council
  • Technogenic Society

Faction is not just flavour — weekly faction blessings apply to one faction rather than to everything, so a deck concentrated in a single faction gets more out of them than a spread one does.

Mana Power-up

Units have a Mana Power-up — an ability you spend mana to trigger during a match, separate from summoning and merging. It is a third thing mana is for, and it is easy to forget it exists.

What it does depends entirely on the unit, and some units rewrite it completely:

  • Banshee uses it to make every Banshee on the board shriek, damaging all monsters and barring bosses and mini bosses from using their abilities for 2 seconds.
  • Bard does not get a classic Mana Power-up at all — the button toggles between two modes instead, and the mode is shared by every Bard.
  • Blazey uses it to extinguish every Blazey at once, converting them into rank and Ash.

So "what does its mana ability do" is a question worth asking of every unit before you build around it.

One carry, four supports

The standard deck shape is one damage unit and four cards that multiply it. Not two damage units and three supports, and definitely not four damage units — one.

The reason sits in the Phantom: in PvP, at the end of each wave your damage is converted directly into the health of the boss your opponent must kill. You are being scored on one big number at one moment, so concentrating everything into a single unit beats spreading it across several.

Co-op has no Phantom, so that particular arithmetic does not apply there — but the shape holds for a different reason: one leak ends the run, so you still want the strongest possible board rather than several mediocre ones.

So the question to ask of a candidate card is not "is this card good?" but "what does this do for my carry?" Raise its damage, raise its attack speed, or keep the wave off it. A card that does none of those is a bad card for this deck, whatever colour its border is.

Some units break this rule by wanting copies of themselves rather than support. Blade Dancer scales with every Dancer on the field and peaks at seven; Banshee needs inactive Banshees to feed the active ones; Blazey passes flame between Blazeys and doubles an attack at four or more. Read the ability text before assuming the standard shape applies.

Placement

Most units do not care which tile they land on. The ones that do care a great deal, and their ability text says so explicitly:

  • Distance from the path. Banshee only activates on a tile bordering the path. Off the path she does nothing but feed her rank to the Banshees that are on it.
  • Distance from her own kind. Blade Dancer wants no nearby Dancers for its attack speed bonus, while wanting as many Dancers on the field as possible for its damage bonus — near, but not adjacent.
  • Proximity for spreading. Blazey passes the flame to other Blazeys around her, so clustering is the point.

When one of these lands badly, that is a genuine reason to merge it away.

Reincarnation

Reincarnation is a unique enhancement for units, and it sits at the far end of a unit's development. It unlocks only after a unit has reached both:

  • the maximum level of 15, and
  • the 4th Ascension.

After that, 3 levels of Reincarnation become available.

Each level increases bonus deck damage — and that bonus influences the damage dealt by all units in a deck containing a Reincarnated unit, not just the Reincarnated one. Bonus deck damage stacks from every Reincarnated unit in the deck.

So the payoff is not a stronger card. It is a stronger deck, which is why Reincarnating a unit you do not field can still be worth it if it sits in the deck.

Reincarnated units also unlock successive synergies with other units, where those exist.

Synergized units

Synergy unlocks a Reincarnated unit's new abilities, and which abilities you get depends on other units.

  • Synergized units are a set of summoned units, each of which unlocks one particular synergetic ability of the key unit.
  • A synergetic ability is a unit's unique ability. One is unlocked at each level of Reincarnation.
  • The level and value of that ability's bonus depend on the synergized unit's own Reincarnation level.

If both units are Reincarnated you gain a new ability, and it becomes more powerful with each level of Reincarnation. This is the one place in the game where investment in two cards compounds rather than merely adding up.

Celestial Shards

Celestial Shards are a unique resource that appears where the celestial rifts have been, as fragments carrying the power of transforming matter. They are what lets units gain new abilities through their Reincarnations.

This is an extremely rare and valuable resource. It comes from four places:

  • the Champion Store
  • the Clan Store
  • Dragon Rifts
  • unique events of the Isle of Rhandum

See also celestial shards in the resource list.
