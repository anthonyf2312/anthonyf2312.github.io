PvP

Real-time, head to head. Both players get an identical board and face the same waves. Each starts with 3 hearts: an ordinary monster reaching the gate costs 1, a boss costs 2. You lose when they run out.

You attack the other player indirectly, by killing things. Every monster you kill — bosses aside — respawns out of your opponent's portal as a common monster. Kill faster and you bury them in traffic; stall and the traffic is all yours.

Each round ends with a Phantom appearing on your board. The damage your units deal to it is converted into the health of the boss that appears on your opponent's path — so the boss they have to survive is one you built. How much of your damage carries over depends on the wave number, counting for very little in the opening waves and growing from wave 3 onwards.

Kill your boss before they kill theirs and you take a mana bonus that lasts the rest of the battle, and those bonuses stack.

If both gates fall at the same instant, the tiebreak is whoever has less total monster health left on their path. Clearing the board is the win condition even at the moment you lose it.

This is why decks are built as one carry and four supports: the supports exist to make the carry's hit on the Phantom as large as possible at the one moment it is measured.

PvP is also where trophies come from, which is what decides your arena.

PvP variants

Rush for Glory

You build your deck and pick a hero by repeatedly choosing from three random options. Your collection matters much less here, because you are drafting on the spot rather than bringing what you own.

Mirror Decks

Both players fight with identical, pre-assembled decks. The purest test in the game — nothing separates the two players except how they play the board.

If you feel out-collected rather than outplayed in standard PvP, these two variants are where a newer account can compete on level terms.

The Death Wave

The Death Wave exists to stop long matches going on forever. Once it starts, the usual rhythm breaks down:

  • The monster phase and the boss phase merge. Monsters arrive on a fixed interval and bosses join every 30 seconds, without soaking up the health of whatever is still alive.
  • Nothing crosses to the other side. Both players get identical monsters, at the same time.
  • Enemies gain extra armour and resistance to percentage damage on top of the usual health, and all three keep climbing the longer it runs.

Anything that normally triggers when the last boss of a wave dies now triggers on every boss death, and anything that lasts "during the boss phase" stays active as long as a boss is on the path.

It has moved around. It arrived in August 2024 as a permanent part of every competitive PvP mode except the Rhandum League, beginning after wave 10. In April 2026 it came back as a temporary mode inside classic PvP instead, open from League 1 and starting after wave 7 — earlier, and with stronger monsters.

Because it is now a temporary mode rather than a permanent phase, whether you meet it at all depends on what is running. Check in-game before planning a deck around it.

The other competitive modes

Several modes have been added since the pages above were first written. Each is summarised from the official patch notes rather than from in-game text.

Rhandum League

The one mode where your collection does not matter at all. Every player is given the same levels — commons and rares at 15, epics at 13, legendaries at 11, rare items at level 10 with no enchantments — and both players' critical damage is fixed at 2,000. You build five decks, your opponent blocks two of them and you block two of theirs. Some units are marked "key" and can only appear in one of your five decks. You keep playing until you run out of attempts.

Shard Hunting

Three decks, and each player bans one of the other's before the match. It pays Celestial Shards, which is the reason to play it. A PRO mode for players past 6,000 trophies lets your opponent ban two decks instead of one, and pays triple. Defeats cost a heart, and you get three a day.

Aggrogeddon

A weekend mode that inverts the usual balance: you score Aggro Points for inflicting negative effects on your opponent, and reducing an enemy unit's merge rank pays the most. The more points you bank, the nastier their monsters get and the more mana and spells you receive. There are no bosses — only a Villainous Wizard who fires borrowed boss abilities at you every 30 seconds.

Masters' Tournament

Runs twice a season from Arena 5, alternating with the Mirror Match. You get 12 pre-built decks. Units you do not own are played at level 7; units you do own are capped at level 10, so a maxed collection is no advantage. The rewards are Mastery Badges — silver for three straight wins with one deck, gold for five — and they sit on your profile.

Supremacy Tournament

The top-end mode, open only to players who passed 7,600 trophies the previous season. Seven days, three decks with mutual bans, and a modifier that changes every day, so decks that worked yesterday may not today. Scored in Medals; pays economic and combat perks.

Challenge of Hunters

Previously the Challenge of Seven. A one-hour sprint for a group of 15, scored by how many of three randomly chosen bosses you kill — and kills count even in matches you lose. Free to enter.

Star of the Week

A featured unit rotates weekly, always from one of the factions currently blessed. Wins with it in your deck score points towards a leaderboard.

Arenas

Your trophy count places you in an arena, and climbing does not just mean tougher opponents — it introduces new enemies. The Virus boss starts appearing in PvP from arena 7, and the Assassin from arena 9.

Worth knowing in advance, because both reward having thought about your deck rather than discovering them mid-match.

Co-op

Two players clearing waves of AI enemies together. It shares the board and the deck rules with PvP and almost nothing else — the two things that define a PvP match are both absent here.

One gate, and it is sudden death

There is no gate each. There is one gate between the two of you, and no hearts:

If one monster reaches the gate, the game finishes. There is no heart to lose and no second chance — a single leak ends the run for both players.

Nothing is sent anywhere

There is no Phantom in co-op, and no opponent to feed. Your kills stay dead instead of respawning on someone else's board, so damage here has exactly one job: kill what is in front of you before it walks past. Killing a monster gives mana to both players.

That flips the emphasis completely. PvP rewards one enormous hit at the end of a wave, because that hit is your attack; co-op has nowhere to send damage, so a burst deck that goes quiet between waves is simply a deck with gaps. A single leak is instantly fatal here, which makes consistent coverage worth more than peak damage.

Chapters, dungeons and floors

Co-op is not an open-ended run of numbered waves. It is structured:

  • Each chapter is a separate dungeon.
  • Each dungeon has several floors.
  • A floor is a battlefield where you defeat several bosses and the waves of monsters ahead of them.

Floors and chapters are completed in a specific order, so there is a fixed path through the content rather than a difficulty dial. You can return to floors you have already completed.

The final floor of a dungeon is Endless, and has no defined waves. It is the one floor that does not end on its own — how long you last is the score.

Modifiers and boosts

Each season has its own dungeon modifier, which becomes active after the 5th floor. On top of that, each floor carries its own negative modifiers and offers a selection of boosts to choose from.

The Endless floor is the exception: the choices available there are different from the ones on ordinary floors.

The in-game text points at the Knowledge Base for the Endless floor's specific choices rather than listing them, so they are not reproduced here. This is a gap worth filling from the game.

Treasure Maps and rewards

Treasure Maps are used before you start a run, and earn you more gold for your achievements in it. There are two types; the source does not say what distinguishes them, so that is not guessed at here.

Completing each floor and each chapter for the first time awards Treasure Chests. Replaying a cleared floor does not repeat that reward.

The Endless floor pays out on time survived instead. The one worked example the game gives: 8 minutes earns 1650 gold.

The two roles

Co-op teams generally split into DPS and support:

  • DPS brings the damage and does the killing.
  • Support brings cards that amplify the teammate's units, control the flow of waves, or manipulate mana generation — and often contributes very little damage directly.

Two DPS players is the classic co-op failure: plenty of damage, nothing holding the waves together. Agree the roles before you start.

Clans

A clan is a group of players who chat, help each other with cards, and unlock access to clan-only content — chiefly Dragon Rift and the Clan Tournament. Joining one is close to free power, since both of those are content you cannot reach alone.

Clans open up at arena 2. Joining one is free; founding your own costs 5,000 coins. A clan holds up to 50 players.

What membership actually buys you:

  • Clan chat.
  • Requesting common and rare cards you are short of — and earning coins by donating cards to other members.
  • Training battles and co-op with people you know.
  • Entry to the Clan Tournament.

Dragon Rift

A clan-based PvE mode. Members progress through a series of worlds, and at the end of each world the clan unlocks and battles a dragon. Every member's victories chip away at the same dragon's health, and when it runs out the clan moves on to the next world.

Because progress is cumulative across the whole clan, consistent small contributions from everyone matter more than one strong player.

Clan War

The clan event is the Clan War. It runs every other week after a season starts, and it lives in the Events section rather than in the clan screen. Your clan needs 10 players to take part.

You earn Glory Points by completing clan quests, which refresh daily. How many points a quest pays scales with your trophy count. Points move your clan's hot air balloon up towards a treasure key; clans that reach the key and get the balloon airborne are paid, and the winner is whichever clan has the most Glory Points once every day has been counted.

A clan's group — what used to be called its division — decides how many Clan Coins its quests and placings pay out.

What changed, and why older guides disagree

This replaced the old Clan Tournament in February 2025, and the change was not cosmetic. Daily Battles and Plunder are gone, replaced by the daily clan quests above. Sandals, healing potions, boss defenders and tournament spells were all part of the old structure, and the patch notes say spells and defenders were set aside rather than carried across. If a guide describes a four-phase tournament with a rating week and plundering, it is describing the pre-2025 game.

Clan Collection

Placing well in a Clan War also pays collection items — there are 8 to gather, and you can trade them with clanmates directly in clan chat. A full set is worth 25 tokens, spent in the Clan Store.

Where this came from. The Clan War rules above are taken from the official patch notes for updates 28.1 (2025-02-10) and 33.0 (2025-10-30), not from the in-game text — so the shape of the mode is right, but treat any specific figure as unconfirmed until it is checked in-game.

Events

Events are the third mode alongside PvP and co-op, and they live in the fourth tab along the bottom of the screen. They run every weekend and last 48 hours.

The weekly rotation has changed since this was written. The official patch notes say the Mirror Match became the only weekly event format in June 2025, with leftover Royal Trials and Rush for Glory tickets converted into it; the Masters' Tournament then joined it in October 2025, the two alternating through a season. The Rush for Glory scoring below has not been rechecked in-game, so treat it as the format's rules rather than as this week's event.

Rush for Glory

You are sorted into one of 10 groups by trophy count — past 4,000 trophies you are always in group 10, the highest. You get 10 games to bank as many Glory Points as you can:

ResultGlory Points
A win1,000
Each life you finish with100
Each monster left on the enemy's side1
A loss100

Each group holds 100 players, and the top 3 take the best rewards. Note the scoring: winning with lives intact is worth far more than winning narrowly, and a loss is not worth nothing — playing all 10 games beats protecting a record.
