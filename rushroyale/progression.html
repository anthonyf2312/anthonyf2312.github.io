Card levels

A card's level is permanent and lives outside matches. Raising it takes duplicate copies of that card plus gold — and for legendaries, magic orbs as well, at every level.

Do not confuse card level with board rank. Rank comes from merging during a match and resets when the match ends. Level is what you actually own. See merging.

This duplicate-plus-currency shape is why rarity matters: rarer cards produce duplicates more slowly, so they climb more slowly even when you have the gold.

Heroes

A hero is the character behind your deck. Levelling one does three separate things, and the second is easy to miss:

  • The hero itself gets stronger.
  • Every 4th hero level raises your account's critical damage — a benefit that applies no matter which hero or deck you play.
  • It unlocks more item slots, up to four.

Expeditions

Expeditions are a passive, constantly running source of hero fragments — they tick along without you playing matches, and can turn up legendary heroes for gold. If you check nothing else daily, check these.

Legendarity

Legendarity is an account-wide stat, not a currency and not something you can buy directly. It is calculated from the raw levels of your legendary units and your heroes, and it rises every time one of those levels up.

What it is for: legendarity unlocks the spells and artifacts you can equip to a deck and use in battle. So it is the mechanism that turns investment in legendaries into an ability rather than just a bigger number.

Because it derives from levels rather than from how many legendaries you own, committing hard to a few legendaries raises legendarity faster than collecting many and levelling none.

Talents

Talents are unlockable upgrades attached to cards, which you then level. Talent acquisition is tied to legendarity progression, so it advances as your heroes and legendary units do.

They are not optional flavour — staying competitive at higher arenas assumes you have been unlocking and levelling them along the way.

Spells and artifacts

Both are abilities you equip to a deck and use in battle, and both are gated behind legendarity. They sit alongside your five cards rather than replacing any of them, which makes them a second axis of deckbuilding once you have enough legendarity to have a choice.

What to level first

With everything competing for the same gold, a reasonable order:

  1. The five cards in your actual deck. Nothing else changes a match you are playing today.
  2. Your hero. The crit bonus every 4th level and the extra item slots apply to every deck you will ever build, so this never becomes wasted.
  3. Two or three legendaries, deeply. Orbs are the constraint, and depth raises legendarity while breadth does not.
  4. Talents on the cards you have committed to.

The tempting order — level whatever just dropped — is the one that leaves you with a wide, shallow collection and no deck strong enough to climb with.
