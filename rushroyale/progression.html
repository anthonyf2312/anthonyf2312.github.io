RUSH ROYALE · PROGRESSION

Getting
stronger

Three separate ladders run at once — cards, heroes, and the account stat they feed. Understanding which is which stops you spending on the wrong one.

Last reviewed 2026-08-30 · Helped with Claude Code

Card levels

A card's level is permanent and lives outside matches. Raising it takes duplicate copies of that card plus gold — and for legendaries, magic orbs as well, at every level.

Do not confuse card level with board rank. Rank comes from merging during a match and resets when the match ends. Level is what you actually own. See merging.

This duplicate-plus-currency shape is why rarity matters: rarer cards produce duplicates more slowly, so they climb more slowly even when you have the gold.

Heroes

A hero is the character behind your deck. Levelling one does two separate things, and the second is easy to miss:

  • The hero itself gets stronger, and each level issues ability points to spend on its abilities.
  • Every 4th hero level raises your account's critical damage — a benefit that applies no matter which hero or deck you play.

Summoning and levelling a hero

Heroes are summoned with fragments, which come from the battle pass, the shop, or Hero's Chests. Once summoned they level up, unlocking abilities and issuing ability points as they go.

The ceiling is level 100. It has moved twice: it was 20 for years, went to 80 in October 2024 — with everyone's existing hero levels multiplied by four — and to 100 that December. Some abilities gain an extra effect once you have invested 80 ability points.

Which heroes there are

Heroes are graded by rarity like units, but rarity here sets how many fragments a hero needs rather than how strong it is. There are 15.

Two things worth knowing about that list. The Captain is the hero you start with — he took over that role from the Trainer in March 2024, keeping the same abilities. And Flicker, the baby dragon, was originally obtainable only through the Dragon Rift; he has since turned up in epic hero fragments and the Expedition shop.

Separately there are event heroes, which are only available while their event runs and cannot be obtained afterwards: Jake Paul (2023), and PAC-MAN and Ducky from the arcade crossover in March 2026.

SIMM-21 is the odd one out. It is a defensive hero — its abilities spawn a bot, shield and cleanse your units, and cut the damage bosses deal — and the developers described it in June 2026 as good at protecting a board but weaker overall than the other legendary heroes. It is listed here because it demonstrably exists, but see the note below.

Where this came from, and one gap. The roster and the level cap are taken from the official patch notes, not from in-game text. The names and rarities are stated there directly; nothing about their abilities is recorded here, because the notes describe changes rather than what each hero currently does.

SIMM-21 has no introduction anywhere. Across all 83 posts in both official news archives it is named exactly once, in a June 2026 balance post that reworks it. Whatever announced it was never published to either feed, so how you obtain it is unknown — treat this entry as evidence the hero exists and nothing more.

Ability points

Ability points are issued for levelling up your hero, and they are what you spend to enhance that hero's abilities.

This replaces the old equipment system outright. Heroes no longer have item slots, and there are no craftable items to put in them — a hero's power now comes from its level and the points that level pays out. What you equip to a deck is a separate system: see spells, artifacts & perks.

Critical damage

Critical damage is a shared, account-wide multiplier — one number for all your units, shown in the Cards tab next to your deck. The base chance of a critical hit is 5%, and talents, certain units and weekly faction blessings can raise it.

The multiplier itself goes up every time you level a unit, a hero, or a defender in your tournament defence. That is worth knowing: levelling a card you never play still makes the cards you do play hit harder.

Expeditions

Expeditions send heroes out to earn hero fragments and other rewards. They run on timers rather than on matches, so they tick along whether or not you are playing — which is what makes them worth opening daily.

You get up to 5 portals, and how many depends on your Division; some stay chained until you reach a given one (Division 5 and Division 7 are the examples given). A portal running an expedition shows a countdown; an empty one shows when its next expedition appears. They refresh at the same time as daily quests.

There are three tiers — Regular, Great and Legendary — differing in how long they take, with Regular around 4 hours. The higher your Division, the more often the better tiers come up.

There is also an Expedition Shop selling hero fragments: daily offers of small bundles for crystals, larger weekly bundles that are often discounted, and seasonal premium bundles for real money.

Legendarity

Legendarity is an account-wide stat, not a currency and not something you can buy directly. It is received for upgrading legendary units and heroes, and it rises every time one of those is upgraded.

It does two jobs. High legendarity shows your status, and it unlocks access to spells, artifacts and profile components. The higher your legendarity, the more choices you have — so it is the mechanism that turns investment in legendaries into options rather than just a bigger number.

Because it comes from upgrading rather than from how many legendaries you own, committing hard to a few legendaries raises legendarity faster than collecting many and upgrading none.

What it unlocks lives on its own page: see spells, artifacts & perks.

One change to watch for. The official patch notes for March 2026 say epic units and heroes were shifted to give less critical damage and legendarity instead, and that Division 7 now unlocks on legendarity rather than critical damage. If those hold, legendarity has quietly become the more important of the two numbers.

The Pantheon and faction bonuses

Three separate bonuses attach to units rather than to your account. They are easy to confuse because they stack on the same new release.

The Pantheon

The Pantheon holds five units, one per faction, and only damage-type units are eligible. Holding a seat grants a permanent damage bonus — the notes put it at 15%. When a new damage unit of that faction arrives it takes the seat, and the unit it displaces loses the bonus.

This is the game's way of rotating the meta without nerfing anything: a unit does not get worse, it just stops being the one holding the seat.

Multifaction and Novelty

A newly released unit carries two temporary bonuses, and since April 2026 they run on separate clocks:

BonusLastsWhat it gives
Multifaction about 1 month Every faction blessing at once, the highest-level faction perks, and entry to every faction quest and leaderboard simultaneously
Novelty about 2 months 10% more damage

Both expire, and when they do the unit settles back into its own faction. That is why a new legendary can feel dramatically stronger in its first weeks than it does later — the unit did not change, its bonuses ran out.

Weekly faction blessings

Two factions are blessed each week. Units of those factions pay extra rewards for killing bosses, and there is a leaderboard attached that resets on the same cycle. This is the bonus the Star of the Week rotates in step with.

Where this came from. This whole section is summarised from the official patch notes for updates 30.0, 34.0, 35.0 and 35.2 rather than from in-game text. The structure is stated there plainly; the two percentages are the only figures given, and neither has been checked in-game.

Talents

Talents are unlockable upgrades attached to cards, which you then level. Talent acquisition is tied to legendarity progression, so it advances as your heroes and legendary units do.

They are not optional flavour — staying competitive at higher arenas assumes you have been unlocking and levelling them along the way.

Spells and artifacts

Both are abilities you equip to a deck and use in battle, and both are unlocked by gaining legendarity. They sit alongside your five cards rather than replacing any of them, which makes them a second axis of deckbuilding once you have enough legendarity to have a choice.

You take one spell and up to three artifacts into battle. Spells, artifacts & perks covers the archetypes, relics, slots and profile perks in full.

What to level first

With everything competing for the same gold, a reasonable order:

  1. The five cards in your actual deck. Nothing else changes a match you are playing today.
  2. Your hero. The crit bonus every 4th level and the ability points apply to every deck you will ever build, so this never becomes wasted.
  3. Two or three legendaries, deeply. Orbs are the constraint, and depth raises legendarity while breadth does not.
  4. Talents on the cards you have committed to.

The tempting order — level whatever just dropped — is the one that leaves you with a wide, shallow collection and no deck strong enough to climb with.
